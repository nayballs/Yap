Yap

Privacy

Last updated 5 October 2026

Yap is a voice dictation app that runs on your computer, and you don't need an account to use it. This policy covers the optional Yap account service at auth.contextmirror.com, run by Nathan Lawrie ("we"), and how the Yap app handles a calendar you connect.

The short version

Your voice, transcripts, dictation history, notes, settings and any calendar you connect stay on your PC. The account service never receives them. It knows who you are so you can sign in, and nothing more. If you never sign in, Yap only contacts it when you open the Account page, to see which ways to sign in are available.

What we collect

  • Your email address.
  • Your name and profile picture link, if you sign in with Google, GitHub or Discord and they share them.
  • An ID from that provider that links your account to theirs. We never see your password for those services.
  • Sign-in sessions: when you signed in, plus the IP address and app or browser of that device, so sessions can be ended and abuse spotted.
  • Sign-in codes we email you, stored only in scrambled (hashed) form and deleted once used or after 10 minutes.
  • Phone sign-in requests, if you sign in with your phone: the code Yap shows and which account approved it. Each is deleted once Yap finishes signing in; unfinished ones are cleared out after they expire (codes last 10 minutes).
  • Where a phone sign-in request came from, so your phone can show you before you approve it: the approximate city and country of the PC that asked (Cloudflare's estimate from its IP address), the Yap version it runs, and a scrambled (hashed) network identifier that only tells whether your phone is on the same network. The IP address itself isn't stored. Kept for about 70 minutes.
  • A mark on sign-ins made with your phone: a scrambled (hashed) form of that session's key, kept for 2 days, so that in its first day such a sign-in can't delete your account or sign out your other devices.
  • Rate-limit counters tied to IP addresses for a few minutes, to block automated abuse.
  • A record of sign-in code requests: scrambled (hashed) forms of the email address and IP address that asked, kept for 24 hours, so nobody can flood an inbox with codes.

How we use it

Only to sign you in and keep you signed in, to send you sign-in codes and essential account emails, and to protect the service from abuse. When you approve a sign-in with your phone, we email you that Yap on a PC was signed in to your account, with that PC's Yap version and approximate location, so a sign-in you didn't make doesn't go unnoticed. We don't sell your data, use it for advertising, or send marketing email. There are no analytics or advertising cookies.

Who handles it for us

  • Cloudflare hosts the service and its database.
  • Resend delivers our emails.
  • Google, GitHub or Discord, if you choose to sign in with them, handle that step under their own privacy policies.

These companies may process data outside your country under standard contractual safeguards.

Your calendar, in the Yap app

If you connect a calendar in Yap (Settings → Connectors), the Yap app on your PC reads it directly from Google Calendar, or from the private calendar link you paste. The account service never receives your calendar, and it isn't involved.

  • What Yap reads, with Google's read-only permissions to see your calendar list and the events on calendars you own: for the past 12 hours and the next 7 days, each event's title, start and end, location, description, meeting link, organizer, and attendees' names, email addresses and replies.
  • How Yap uses it: only to list your meetings in Yap, remind you before they start, open the meeting link when you ask, and give a meeting note its title, attendees and the invite's description. Yap's AI features then use those as context for that meeting's summary and your questions about it, through the AI provider you set up in Yap (which can be one that runs on your PC); AI apps you let read your meeting notes (Settings → MCP) see the meeting's name and attendees with them. Yap never writes to your calendar.
  • Where it's kept: on your PC only. The meetings are in Yap's data folder; Google's access token, or your calendar link, is in Windows Credential Manager. Nothing is sent to us.
  • Removing it: Disconnect in Settings → Connectors deletes the token or link and that calendar's meetings from your PC (notes you took stay), and withdraws Yap's access at Google. You can also remove Yap's access in your Google Account at any time.

Yap's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Cookies

Only the strictly necessary ones on auth.contextmirror.com: a sign-in session cookie, and short-lived cookies that protect a sign-in in progress and pass it back to the app.

How long we keep it

Your account data stays until you delete your account. App sign-ins expire after 30 days without use. Deleting your account removes your account, its linked sign-ins and every session straight away; copies can remain in encrypted backups for up to 30 days before they're gone too.

Your rights

You can see, correct, export or delete your data. Delete your account in Yap under Settings → Account, or email us for anything else. If you're in the UK or EU you can also complain to your data protection authority, such as the ICO in the UK.

Children

Yap accounts aren't intended for children under 13.

Changes

If this policy changes we'll update this page and its date, and point out significant changes in Yap.
